💡 Understanding Evaluation Metrics

A simple guide to what each performance metric measures in our dog breed classifier

Accuracy

Overall Correctness

Accuracy = Correct Predictions / Total Predictions

Measures the percentage of images correctly classified. On a 120-class dataset, random guessing has an accuracy of only 0.83% (1/120). Achieving 77.64% Top-1 and 96.87% Top-5 indicates excellent discriminatory power.

Precision

Exactness / Quality

Precision = True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives)

Answers: "When the model predicts a dog is a Golden Retriever, how often is it actually a Golden Retriever?" High precision means fewer false alarms.

Recall

Completeness / Sensitivity

Recall = True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives)

Answers: "Out of all actual Golden Retrievers in the test set, how many did the model find?" High recall means the model rarely misses instances of a breed.

F1-Score

Harmonic Balance

F1-Score = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall)

The harmonic mean of precision and recall. It balances precision and recall into a single score, preventing misleading results when classes have varying difficulty.

Empirical confusion matrix evaluation identifying fine-grained visual classification challenges across 120 breeds

💡

Why Are Visually Similar Dog Breeds Misclassified?

Fine-grained image classification on 120 dog breeds is substantially more complex than general object recognition (e.g., distinguishing a dog from a car or bicycle). In our trained MobileNetV2 model, all misclassifications cluster within closely related canine families due to four key machine learning factors:

🧬

High Inter-Class Similarity

Breeds sharing common ancestral lineages (such as Sighthounds, Arctic Spitz dogs, or Welsh Corgis) have almost indistinguishable skeletal frameworks, head contours, and coat textures. Even expert kennel club judges often rely on tactile handling or precise shoulder height measurements that 2D photographs cannot convey.

📐

High Intra-Class Variance

Dogs of the exact same breed exhibit wide variation due to age (puppy vs. adult), coat grooming or clipping styles, fur coloration variants, and photography angles (sitting, running, portrait).

🔍

Dimensional Resolution Bottleneck

CNN inputs are standardized to 224 × 224 pixels. This downsampling can blend subtle micro-textures—such as the exact coarseness of a terrier's coat or slight differences in ear-tip curvature—making two similar breeds appear identical to convolution kernels.

🌳

Perspective, Background & Lighting

In-the-wild user and benchmark photos contain varying depth of field, occluding foliage, shadows, and differing perspectives that obscure diagnostic breed hallmarks (such as tail docking or relative scale).

🎯 Key Insight for Evaluation / Viva: Notice that 100% of the model's top confused pairs are between dogs belonging to the exact same breed family (e.g., Whippet vs Italian Greyhound, Lhasa vs Shih-Tzu, Cardigan vs Pembroke). This proves the model has mastered deep canine morphology and only encounters ambiguity at the extreme limits of visual discriminability!
